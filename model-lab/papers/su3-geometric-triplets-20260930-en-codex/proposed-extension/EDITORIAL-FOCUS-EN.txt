Central question
When does a geometrically degenerate triplet sector support an interacting
dynamics that preserves the relevant subspace and respects its actual
symmetries? Prioritize polarization-dependent exchange and quantitative
subspace invariance. Do not assume U(3) from linear degeneracy alone.

Proposed main-text sequence
1. Linear degeneracy: the exact graph triplets and their established scope.
2. Nonlinear countercheck: the demonstrated quartic symmetry breaking.
3. Exchange together with subspace preservation: exact compatible exchange,
   followed by the independently checked Schur and finite-time leakage
   propositions in extension.tex. Preserve their finite Hermitian scope.
4. Realized control models: compatible artificial ports and the externally
   prescribed radial double-shell Klein–Gordon mechanism, with their
   respective assumptions stated explicitly.
5. One concrete open field bridge: polarization-dependent exchange between
   actual spatial mode sectors, assessed together with quantitative leakage.

Immediate field question: polarization-dependent exchange
The spherical field normal form H4=A[2S²+|T|²], T=a.a, already distinguishes
linear and circular polarization. It must not be conflated with the graph
quartic's cubic anisotropy. The remaining rotation/polarization structure
may support useful interactions without U(3); that possibility is the
question, not a demonstrated new mechanism.
The next missing quantities are the cross-quartic couplings of two actual
spatial mode sectors and their transfer dynamics. Equal norms do not imply
equal energies when polarization contributes to the energy. Compare starts
at the same norm and report their energies as distinct preparations, or
match energies explicitly and account for the resulting amplitude changes.
Report canonical mode populations, spatial energy, leakage, and the total
balance. The isolated normal form does not establish spontaneous
polarization switching.

Quantitative preservation over a transfer time
For realistic field overlaps retain the external modes and examine the
anisotropic blocks, QHP, distance to eliminated-block poles, and error over
the transfer time together. The exact exchange W=JI and the previously
derived compatibility conditions are the reference case. Artificially
matched ports remain positive controls, not a microscopic derivation.
The Schur proposition diagnoses induced anisotropy and bounds the total
correction away from poles. The leakage proposition gives a conservative
finite-time norm bound. Neither independently certifies full transfer
accuracy or nonlinear field closure; keep the assumptions visible.

Self-consistent field bridge: prerequisites before transfer
An explicitly specified extension of the self-consistent field model must
first be checked for two usable positive radial branches. The investigated
Q-ball has not confirmed the required dipole structure. Translation modes
and box states cannot supply the missing branches. Establish their nature
before any transfer calculation. If the branches are absent, the external
shell model remains a valid control. A changed model must be named as such,
without presenting it as a retrospective derivation of the old model.

Separate BIC/resonance continuation
Ask whether weak radiation and finite coherent exchange follow from the
same field operator. Linewidth, frequency splitting, open harmonics, and
energy flux must be derived or measured together. The passive two-mode
example C illustrates logical compatibility only. KRAFT-still does not
establish a perfect force switch; darkness establishes neither absence of
force nor self-binding. Do not mix KRAFT numbers into the triplet controls.
