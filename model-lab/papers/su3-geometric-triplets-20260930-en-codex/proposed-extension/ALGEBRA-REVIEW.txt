A. SCHUR ELIMINATION
Work on a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space with its positive-definite
inner product. H=H^dagger is time-independent. P=P^dagger=P^2 has rank three;
Q=I-P. Blocks are restrictions to Ran(P) and Ran(Q), not full-space matrices:
A=PHP|Ran(P)=lambda I_P, D=QHQ|Ran(Q), V=PHQ:Ran(Q)->Ran(P).
Lambda is real. The full block matrix is [[A,V],[V^dagger,D]].
P need not commute with H. Calling P a spectral projector of the full H
would instead impose V=0 and exclude the proposed nonzero-coupling example.
A triplet of a compressed or reference operator is the appropriate meaning.

For real z not in spec(D), write an eigenvector as p+q. Its equations are
(A-zI_P)p+Vq=0 and V^dagger p+(D-zI_Q)q=0.
The second equation gives q=-(D-zI_Q)^(-1)V^dagger p. Substitution gives
[H_eff(z)-zI_P]p=0,
H_eff(z)=lambda I_P-V(D-zI_Q)^(-1)V^dagger.
Here p cannot be zero for a nonzero eigenvector at such z. Conversely, a
nonzero p in this kernel reconstructs a unique full eigenvector by the same
formula. Equivalently,
det(H-zI)=det(D-zI_Q) det(H_eff(z)-zI_P).
Thus the determinant condition is exact on the stated resolvent domain.
At z in spec(D), this expression cannot be used by substituting into the
inverse; such eigenvalues require a separate full-block analysis. A Q-only
eigenvector can occur at these excluded values when Vq=0. Pole positions
are neither automatically full eigenvalues nor automatically absent.

H_eff(z) is Hermitian for real z on this domain. It is an energy-dependent
Schur operator. It must not silently be treated as a single autonomous
three-dimensional Hamiltonian generating P exp(-iHt) P. Eliminating Q in
time generally produces memory and dependence on Q initial data.

COUNTEREXAMPLE
Let dim Ran(Q)=1, D=Delta with real Delta, and V=g e_1. Then
H_eff(z)-lambda I_P=-|g|^2/(Delta-z) diag(1,0,0).
The source's g^2 is correct if g is stipulated real. For arbitrary complex
g, |g|^2 is required by Hermiticity. For nonzero g and real z != Delta,
this is not scalar; the two unaffected channels have eigenvalue lambda
while the first channel has a nonzero Schur shift. Hence compression
A=lambda I_P alone does not protect scalar effective response.
For additional verification, the full spectrum consists of lambda twice
and (lambda+Delta +/- sqrt((lambda-Delta)^2+4|g|^2))/2.
If lambda=Delta, the two exact lambda eigenvalues are at an excluded
resolvent pole; this illustrates why the determinant-domain caveat matters.
No large-detuning or weak-coupling approximation is needed for this example.

NORM BOUND
With the induced Hilbert-space operator norm and
delta=dist(z,spec(D))>0, the spectral theorem gives
||(D-zI_Q)^(-1)||=1/delta. Submultiplicativity and
||V^dagger||=||V|| imply
||H_eff(z)-lambda I_P|| <= ||V||^2/delta.
This upper-bounds the entire shift. It does not lower-bound anisotropy,
force splitting, or prove the existence of a self-consistent full eigenvalue
inside a prescribed interval. The distance concerns spec(D), not spec(H).
At poles the displayed bound is unavailable. Isotropic nonzero shifts are
possible, for example with equivalent coupled triplets in P and Q.

SUFFICIENT SYMMETRY PROTECTION
Let U(g) be a unitary representation on the full finite-dimensional complex
space, with [U(g),H]=[U(g),P]=0 for every group element. The decomposition
is preserved, D commutes with U_Q(g), and
U_P(g)V=V U_Q(g).
Therefore U_P(g) commutes with V(D-zI_Q)^(-1)V^dagger and H_eff(z).
If U_P is irreducible over the complex numbers, Schur's lemma makes H_eff(z)
scalar on Ran(P). State the full representation and these commuting
conditions; merely naming a triplet or a symmetry of A is insufficient.
This protects scalar response, but does not in general protect closure:
equivalent representations in Q can permit nonzero V and leakage.
Closure is separately characterized by QHP=0 (and, by Hermiticity, PHQ=0).

B. FINITE-TIME LEAKAGE
Use the same H and orthogonal P, and units hbar=1. The bound itself does
not require rank(P)=3 or A=lambda I_P. Let ||psi(0)||=1 and Qpsi(0)=0.
Unitary evolution gives ||psi(t)||=1 for every real t. Set p=Ppsi and q=Qpsi;
then i q_dot=Dq+V^dagger p. Variation of constants gives exactly
q(t)=-i integral_0^t exp[-iD(t-s)] V^dagger p(s) ds.
The exponential is unitary on Ran(Q), and ||p(s)||<=1. Consequently,
||q(t)|| <= |t| ||V^dagger|| = |t| ||QHP||.
The integral remains oriented when t<0; the triangle inequality bounds it
by the integral over [t,0], of length |t|. The separate normalization bound
||q(t)||<=1 then gives
||Qpsi(t)|| <= min(1, |t| ||QHP||),
||Qpsi(t)||^2 <= min(1, t^2 ||QHP||^2).
If physical hbar is retained, replace |t| ||QHP|| by
|t| ||QHP||/hbar throughout.

QHP=0 is sufficient for exact invariance of Ran(P) at all times; it is also
necessary for invariance of that entire subspace, by differentiating at
t=0 for arbitrary initial vectors in Ran(P). A particular state can remain
nonleaking even if QHP is nonzero. The bound is conservative and does not
assert monotone leakage, a sharp lifetime, spectral isolation, or nonlinear
stability. In a classical amplitude model its squared norm is a leakage
fraction in the chosen Hilbert norm; calling it a probability presupposes
the quantum single-state interpretation. General indefinite norms, open
systems, time-dependent projections, and nonlinear fields need separate
arguments. A tolerated fraction epsilon in [0,1] is guaranteed for
|t| ||QHP|| <= sqrt(epsilon), without implying that leakage must reach
epsilon when this sufficient condition fails.

C. OPTIONAL TWO-MODE EXAMPLE
Assume omega,J,gamma,gamma12 are real and gamma>=|gamma12|. Write the proposed
matrix as H_coh-i Gamma/2, with H_coh=omega I+J sigma_x and
Gamma=gamma I+gamma12 sigma_x. Gamma is positive semidefinite, so the model
is passive: d||psi||^2/dt=-psi^dagger Gamma psi <= 0.
Both matrices are diagonalized by the normalized symmetric and
antisymmetric vectors. Eigenvalues are
omega+J-i(gamma+gamma12)/2,
omega-J-i(gamma-gamma12)/2.
For gamma12=gamma, the antisymmetric eigenvalue is real, omega-J; a
nonzero coherent coupling J is algebraically compatible with a dark
channel. Gamma eigenvalues are norm-squared decay rates, whereas amplitude
decay rates are half as large. If gamma=gamma12=0 both channels are dark.
The algebra by itself imposes no microscopic response kernel and proves no
spatial force, physical realization, project-specific radiation rate, or
nonlinear many-body interaction. It is an effective linear coherent-coupling
example; terminology should preserve that limitation.
