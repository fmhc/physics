Literature: limited additional source review

1. Xie/Saffin/Zhou, 2101.06988
https://arxiv.org/html/2101.06988
A reviewer read the model/preparation and sections 4–5; the coordinator
also read sections 4–5. The sextic reference case g=0.5 matches the project's
U=S-S²+0.5S³. Free Q-ball/anti-Q-ball superpositions form long-lived
charge-swapping states; planar quadrupoles and higher three-dimensional
multipoles are also considered. The energy envelope can be nearly spherical
while the charge density swaps in a multipolar pattern. This is not evidence
of spatially orbiting 2+2 pairs. At g=0.5, higher multipoles lose their charge
structure relatively rapidly; 3D lifetimes are shorter than 2D lifetimes.
Figure 22 shows 2D patterns, not the project's 3D solution.

2. Hou/Saffin/Xie/Zhou, 2202.08392
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08392
A reviewer examined the model and sections 4.2–4.4; the coordinator read
the abstract. The potential is logarithmic and therefore different.
Very long lifetimes are observed even for higher charge-swapping composites;
these are finite lower bounds, not a proof of eternal stability. Reflection
conditions in long symmetric runs limit conclusions about symmetry-breaking
perturbations. The authors overlap with reference 1: this is not an
independent replication.

3. Loiko/Perapechka/Shnir, 2012.01052
https://arxiv.org/html/2012.01052
A reviewer examined sections II–IV; the coordinator read the abstract.
The gauged Friedberg–Lee–Sirlin model includes an additional real field and
electromagnetic dynamics. It supports chains with 2/4/6 constituents and
stationary axisymmetric tori carrying angular momentum, not four orbiting
point bodies. Multi-torus states are energy saddle points; no general
stability proof is provided. Applying this construction would require
an explicit change to the project model.

4. Brihaye/Hartmann, 0711.1969v2
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.1969
The coordinator examined the introduction, model/ansatz, and stability
discussion; the text was already in the local source collection. The model
has two complex fields with separate U(1) charges and a density coupling.
It considers stationary nonrotating/rotating components and angularly excited
forms. Numerical existence and energetic comparisons do not replace a full
dynamical stability analysis. This is not new evidence for 2+2 orbital motion.

5. Almumin et al., 2302.11589
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-024-12712-x
The coordinator examined the introduction, end of section 4, and section 5.
Small spatially rotating perturbations are constructed perturbatively;
higher orders can produce radiation. The leading l=1 radial form contains
f', so its distinction from translation/centre-of-mass motion must be checked
independently before including it in the project's internal mode spectrum.
It certifies neither the missing positive dipole mode nor a stable free
four-body composite.

Coordinator's implications for the paper
Reference 1 is the closest new comparison: the same potential, but different
initial data and a different total-charge sector from the project's single
charged Q-ball. Positive/negative charge regions must not be presented as
positive/negative electric charges. Four charge lobes, four energy cores,
and four particle trajectories are different observables.

A useful future research question is whether such a charge pattern survives
or decays after adding a small amount of angular momentum. This would require
new 3D initial data, absorber and volume checks, and asymmetric perturbations.
No such calculation was performed here. A literature match does not authorize
a simulation. There is no demonstration that the project's BIC and a
charge-swapping Q-ball share the same mechanism.
